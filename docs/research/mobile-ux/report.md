# Make the fragment, not the bubble, primary

The Nota Bene Android app already matches or beats the category on several hard things: the full command in the permission sheet, Telegram-style hold, lock and cancel voice recording, a pending-comment review pane, and an 840 dp three-pane threshold that equals Android's "expanded" window class. Its core interaction model points the wrong way, though. Text selection, the one thing that turns an agent reply into something you can act on, sits two steps deep behind a long-press menu that targets the whole message. The research supports the two device-testing findings: selection should be the default, and fragment-level actions (comment, quote, label) should become the primary way to answer an agent, with the whole-message menu kept as a secondary path. Telegram is the only major app with a primary-sourced fragment quote. ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini users keep asking for exactly that on mobile, and no product splits one AI answer into separately actionable messages. Beyond selection, the largest gaps against current coding-agent companions are four. There are no notifications for "needs approval" or "turn finished". Server errors are dropped silently. Pairing has a fingerprint step with no way to reject a mismatch. Pane swiping competes with code blocks, selection handles and the system back gesture. Accessibility is mostly default: the whole app has two `Semantics` widgets. The main implementation risk of the recommended direction is Flutter's `SelectionArea` inside a reversed lazy list and a `PageView`. The issue tracker shows a stream of fixed and still-open bugs in that area, so the direction has to be proved on a device before it is committed.

## Fragment actions win because no one has made the bubble work

**Messaging apps put whole-message actions behind long-press and make partial selection a second step.** In Telegram you tap a message (press and hold on iOS), then hold and drag to select. The selection toolbar adds "Quote", the quote appears above the composer, and tapping it later jumps with an animation to the exact place in the original ([Telegram](https://telegram.org/blog/reply-revolution)). WhatsApp has partial selection on iOS. On Android it was still in beta in mid 2025 and tied to a redesigned context menu, and the notes could not confirm that it has shipped ([WABetaInfo](https://wabetainfo.com/whatsapp-beta-for-android-2-25-18-3-whats-new/)). Reading and annotation apps take the other route: the text itself is selectable and the selection becomes a highlight or note. Readwise Reader can even turn a selection into a highlight in one step ("auto-highlighting") as a user toggle ([Readwise](https://docs.readwise.io/reader/docs/faqs/highlights-tags-notes)). Hypothesis offers Annotate or Highlight from one selection ([Hypothesis](https://web.hypothes.is/help/annotation-basics/)).

**AI chat is where the messaging model fails.** On the Claude app, user reports say copy takes the whole message and selective copy is impossible. A report from a Remote Control session, the same product class as Nota Bene, says selected text copies incorrectly ([report 75155](https://claudeissues.com/issue/75155-feature-request-selective-text-copy-within-messages-mobile-app-desktop-not-only), [report 71718](https://claudeissues.com/issue/71718-bug-cannot-copy-text-correctly-from-code-tab-remote-control-session-on-mobile-ap)). An iPad report says selecting in a long remote session scrolls to the top and deselects ([report 72190](https://claudeissues.com/issue/72190-ipados-app-selecting-text-in-a-long-remote-macos-session-auto-scrolls-to-top-and)). OpenAI's forum is full of 2026 requests for highlight-to-ask, quote-reply and anchored annotations. Support answered without a timeline ([OpenAI forum](https://community.openai.com/t/feature-request-highlight-text-in-responses-to-ask-follow-up-questions-in-a-pop-up-box/1388090), [quote-reply request](https://community.openai.com/t/feature-suggestion-quote-reply-to-specific-text-segments/1378659), [anchored annotations](https://community.openai.com/t/feature-request-inline-anchored-annotations-and-contextual-threads-for-chatgpt-responses/1390620)). One request says users "may need to copy and paste the text or explain which section they mean." Agent replies are long and cover several topics, so a reply to "the whole bubble" pushes that disambiguation back onto the user. That is the device-testing finding, stated by a competitor's user base.

**Nota Bene today follows the messaging model and is clumsier than it.** A long press on a final message opens an overlay with emoji labels and the menu Comment, Select text, Copy, Reply (`mobile/app/lib/chat/message_menu.dart:229-277`). "Select text" then swaps in a `SelectionArea`, selects everything, and re-selects the pressed word by replaying synthetic pointer events with a long-press timer and up to 30 retry frames (`mobile/app/lib/chat/message_selection.dart:80-113`). The selection toolbar offers Comment, Copy and Select all, but no Reply or Quote (`mobile/app/lib/chat/message_selection.dart:172-231`). Reply exists only for the whole message and inserts a Markdown blockquote of all of it (`mobile/app/lib/chat/message_menu.dart:40-42`). Code and diff screens put Comment in the top bar and keep it disabled until something is selected, with no hint (`mobile/app/lib/chat/code_screen.dart:343-349`, `mobile/app/lib/files/diff_screen.dart:291-295`). So the same action lives in three different places.

### Option A (split answers into units) against option B (fragment actions primary)

The device testing left two candidate fixes for "replying to a whole bubble is of little use". The table below weighs them on the evidence in the notes.

| Criterion | A: split a long answer into actionable units (section, paragraph, block) | B: default selection, fragment actions primary |
|---|---|---|
| Precedent | None: **no app splits one AI answer into separate messages**, and no study compares chunked with monolithic answers. Perplexity's layered answer (answer, sources, follow-ups) is the nearest pattern, described only by third-party teardowns ([925 Studios](https://www.925studios.co/blog/perplexity-design-breakdown)) | Telegram's select-then-Quote with jump-back ([Telegram](https://telegram.org/blog/reply-revolution)); reading apps' select-then-note ([Readwise](https://docs.readwise.io/reader/docs/faqs/highlights-tags-notes)) |
| User demand | Indirect: NN/g's chatbot study (8 participants) found users get lost scrolling long replies. The study is known only from secondary write-ups ([Experientia summary](https://blog.experientia.com/?p=20115), [Nielsen on "apple-picking"](https://jakobnielsenphd.substack.com/p/ux-news-20230925)) | Direct: repeated selection and quote requests for ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini (sources above) |
| Precision | Paragraph granularity only; a comment on one identifier inside a paragraph still needs selection | Any span, down to a word; meets device finding (1) |
| Robustness | High: plain tap targets, no platform selection bugs | Exposed to Flutter `SelectionArea` bugs (next section) |
| Accessibility | Good: each unit can carry a TalkBack custom action | Selection alone is not accessible: Hypothesis's selection adder failed screen-reader testing (secondary report), and Android asks for custom-action alternatives to gesture-only actions ([Android accessibility](https://developer.android.com/guide/topics/ui/accessibility/principles)) |
| Cost and parity | Render-time splitting is cheap, because each agent piece is already its own list item (`mobile/app/lib/chat/chat_screen.dart:669-711`). Generation-time splitting would change the protocol, desktop parity and the comment batch format | Replaces the synthetic-pointer code instead of adding to it; the comment anchor (rendered text plus occurrence) already exists (`mobile/app/lib/chat/message_selection.dart:147-170`) |

**Recommendation: B, with a thin layer of A for accessibility.** Each final agent text piece and each user message gets its own always-on `SelectionArea`. One region per item is the lower-risk choice than one region over the list, because cross-list selection is where the crashes cluster. A long press on a word starts the platform selection. The toolbar, built with `contextMenuBuilder` and `AdaptiveTextSelectionToolbar.buttonItems`, keeps the system items and appends Comment, Quote and a labels entry ([contextMenuBuilder](https://api.flutter.dev/flutter/material/SelectionArea/contextMenuBuilder.html)). Quote places a removable chip above the composer. Tapping the chip, a pending comment or a sent quote scrolls to the passage and flashes it, as Telegram does. That repairs the fragile scroll-to-message routine (`mobile/app/lib/chat/chat_screen.dart:122-161`), which can fail silently and shows no highlight. The whole-message menu moves to an explicit affordance: a small "more" button on the last piece of an agent turn, or a long press on bubble chrome outside text. It keeps whole-message labels, Copy all and Reply. No source documents a chat app that uses this hybrid (long press on text selects, long press on chrome opens a menu), so it is a hypothesis to test. From A, keep only the cheap part: every paragraph or block of a final reply exposes "Comment on this paragraph" and "Quote paragraph" as semantics custom actions. Screen-reader and switch users then have a non-selection route, and sighted users are not handed a second visible system to learn. Do not split answers at generation time.

**Main risk.** Flutter widgets are not selectable by default; selection is opt-in per subtree ([SelectableRegion](https://api.flutter.dev/flutter/widgets/SelectableRegion-class.html)), and the combination this app needs has a bug history. A crash when a selectable that holds a selection edge is removed while scrolled out of view was opened on 6 November 2026 and is still open in 3.47 ([flutter#196102](https://github.com/flutter/flutter/issues/196102)). "Select all" in a `ListView` threw a null-check error ([flutter#124078](https://github.com/flutter/flutter/issues/124078)). Scrolling cleared the selection until mid 2023 ([flutter#120892](https://github.com/flutter/flutter/issues/120892)). Scroll jank inside `ListView`/`PageView` was fixed in 2024 ([flutter#152420](https://github.com/flutter/flutter/issues/152420)). The feed is a reversed `ListView.builder` whose streaming item grows in place (`mobile/app/lib/chat/chat_screen.dart:183-196`), inside a `PageView` that turns pages on any horizontal drag (`mobile/app/lib/chat/panes.dart:220-306`). The only documented rule for horizontal drag inside a `PageView` covers iOS `TextField`, not selection handles ([flutter PR 147341](https://www.github.com/flutter/flutter/pull/147341)). Whether a handle drag loses to a page turn has to be tested. The mitigation pattern already exists in the code: while recording, the pager is switched to `NeverScrollableScrollPhysics` (`mobile/app/lib/chat/panes.dart:233-239`). Apply the same lock while a selection is active, and do not make streaming text selectable until it is final (it is already not commentable, `mobile/app/lib/comments/chat_anchor.dart:14-24`).

**What would change the recommendation.** Switch to A as the primary model (visible per-paragraph targets with Comment and Quote, and selection kept only inside a focused full-screen reader) if any of these happens. On-device tests show lost selections, crashes or page turns stealing handle drags at a rate users notice. Pending-comment quotes turn out to cover whole paragraphs most of the time. TalkBack users become a primary audience. The opposite signal would remove the paragraph layer: users never use it and the custom actions go unused.

## Coding-agent companions set a supervision baseline the app partly misses

**By late 2026 every major coding agent has a phone surface built on the same loop**: a session list, a streaming chat, a push when the agent finishes or needs input, approve or deny, and steering by follow-up. Claude's Remote Control keeps execution local, pairs by QR, reconnects after the machine sleeps and sends push notifications on completion or a needed decision ([Claude Code mobile](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/mobile)). Codex in the ChatGPT app pairs by QR, carries the host's Ed25519 key in the code and uses an end-to-end encrypted relay. That description comes from a third-party analysis, not OpenAI docs ([analysis](https://codex.danielvaughan.com/2026/05/15/codex-mobile-chatgpt-app-relay-architecture-remote-agent-control/)). Cursor's iOS app has an inbox of all agents, push on turn end and Live Activities, and it deliberately has no editor, terminal or file browser ([Cursor](https://cursor.com/docs/cloud-agents/mobile)). GitHub Mobile added live progress notifications for agent sessions in 2026 ([GitHub changelog](https://github.blog/changelog/2026-02-26-github-mobile-track-coding-agent-progress-in-real-time-with-live-notifications)).

**Measured against that loop, Nota Bene's biggest omission is notifications.** It has none, local or push. It also pauses its connections 10 minutes after the app is hidden (`mobile/app/lib/home.dart:228-236`), and the phone UX spec lists push as a non-goal. A pending approval therefore blocks the agent until someone happens to open the app. Android's guidance gives a clear shape for this. Use one channel per event class with user-controlled importance ([channels](https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/views/notifications/channels)). Allow at most three actions, private lock-screen visibility, and cancel stale notifications once they are resolved elsewhere ([notification design](https://developer.android.com/design/ui/mobile/guides/home-screen/notifications)). Android 16 Live Updates exclude chat messages but allow ongoing, user-initiated, time-sensitive progress ([Live Updates](https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/views/notifications/live-update)). A foreground service of type `dataSync` is capped at 6 hours per 24 ([FGS timeout](https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/services/fgs/timeout)), so a held socket cannot replace push. Claude users explicitly ask for Approve and Deny inside the push notification ([report 62458](https://claudeissues.com/issue/62458-feature-request-actionable-approve-deny-on-mobile-push-for-permission-prompts)). Slack shows the multi-device rule: hold the phone push while the user is active on the desktop, with a configurable delay ([Slack](https://slack.com/help/articles/201355156-Configure-your-notifications)). The notes do not settle whether push can run through a self-hosted relay without Google services. That is the first technical question for this gap.

**The permission sheet is right where the leader is wrong, and wrong where it interrupts.** Claude's iOS Remote Control reportedly shows only about the first 100 characters of a command ([report 37235](https://claudeissues.com/issue/37235-feature-remote-control-allow-expanding-permission-prompts-on-ios-to-see-the-full)). Nota Bene shows the whole command in a selectable box (`mobile/app/lib/chat/permission_sheet.dart:126-192`), and that is worth keeping. The sheet, however, auto-opens over whatever the user is doing, including typing or selecting. It offers only Deny and Allow, while the richer "for this session" choices exist only in the feed card (`mobile/app/lib/chat/permission_sheet.dart:67-123`).

**Code and diff viewing need two GitHub-era basics and one newer idea.** GitHub Mobile added a line-wrap toggle (its community's top request), a "Jump To" file control and long-press multi-line selection for comments ([GitHub](https://github.blog/news-insights/product-news/even-better-code-review-in-github-for-mobile/)). In 2024 it made monospace text follow the system font setting ([changelog](https://github.blog/changelog/2024-07-08-whats-new-in-mobile-june-update)). Linear's newer "guided reviews" put a summary first and go section by section, with viewed state synced between phone and desktop ([Linear](https://linear.app/now/mobile-diffs)). Nota Bene's code screen never wraps lines. It sizes rows from a `TextPainter` without the system text scaler, behind a fixed `itemExtent` (`mobile/app/lib/chat/code_screen.dart:227-250`), so large-font users plausibly get clipped rows. That has not been verified on a device. Its pinch scale is neither persisted nor tied to the system font (`mobile/app/lib/ui/pinch_scale.dart:19-20`). The diff view has no file-to-file jump and no viewed state. Every source describes a unified diff on phones, which the app already does correctly.

## Navigation, input and states break platform rules in specific places

**Swiping between panes contradicts Material's own caution.** Material allows tabs to be switched by swiping the content, but warns against pairing that with content that also swipes ([M1 tabs](https://m1.material.io:443/components/tabs.html), [M2 tabs](https://m2.material.io/go/design-tabs)). Those pages are M1 and M2: the current M3 text could not be retrieved. Android also says to keep swipeable views clear of the system gesture insets used by edge back ([edge-to-edge](https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/views/layout/edge-to-edge)). Nota Bene's pager takes over as soon as an inner scroller reaches its edge: code blocks (`mobile/app/lib/chat/code.dart:221-223`), display math, the voice waveform. The Files pane's own Files/Changes/Log tabs are tap-only (`mobile/app/lib/files/files_pane.dart:130-134`). Default selection adds selection handles to the conflict. Keep the pill tabs as the primary switch. Allow page swipes only from the pill strip and bubble gutters, or only when no inner scroller can move. Inset the swipe zone by `MediaQuery.systemGestureInsets`.

**Back behaves two ways.** The header back button leaves the chat from any pane (`mobile/app/lib/chat/chat_header.dart:149`), while system back goes pane by pane (`mobile/app/lib/chat/panes.dart:168-178`). Targeting API 36 is required on Play from 31 August 2026 ([Play](https://developer.android.com/google/play/requirements/target-sdk)). That target makes predictive back the default, and intercepting back suppresses its animation ([Android 16](https://developer.android.com/about/versions/16/behavior-changes-16), [Flutter predictive back](https://docs.flutter.dev/platform-integration/android/predictive-back.md)). The header button should follow the same `PopScope` chain as system back.

**The wide layout uses the right number and the wrong test.** The 840 dp threshold (`mobile/app/lib/chat/panes.dart:75`) is the official start of "expanded". Google notes, though, that phones in landscape are almost all under 480 dp tall and that two panes are impractical at compact height ([window size classes](https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/compose/layouts/adaptive/use-window-size-classes)). At 840 dp the chat pane is about 380 dp, narrower than a phone chat. Choose panes by width and height, and let the side panes collapse.

**Input loses work that best practice keeps.** Telegram keeps drafts on the server, pushes them to all devices and clears them as part of the send ([Telegram drafts](https://core.telegram.org/api/drafts)). Its scheduled queue stays editable until dispatch ([scheduled messages](https://core.telegram.org/api/scheduled-messages)). Nota Bene drops the draft when the chat is left (`mobile/app/lib/chat/chat_route.dart:58,97`). It asks "now or after" on every busy send without remembering the choice (`mobile/app/lib/chat/send_now.dart:11-32`), and it never shows a queued message as queued. For voice, WhatsApp set the standard: preview before send, pause, a waveform and 1.5x/2x playback ([WhatsApp](https://blog.whatsapp.com/making-voice-messages-better)). ChatGPT drew complaints when dictation stopped showing an editable transcript before sending ([OpenAI forum](https://community.openai.com/t/voice-dictation-no-longer-shows-transcribed-text-before-sending-major-usability-regression/1177339)). That matters more for coding instructions, where a mis-heard identifier is costly. The app's hold-release sends at once, and playback has no speed control (`mobile/app/lib/voice/voice_view.dart:83-88`). Its outbox, which lets a voice send outlive the chat (`mobile/app/lib/chat/window_tab.dart:99-103`), and the TalkBack tap path into a locked recording (`mobile/app/lib/voice/record_button.dart:212-220`) are ahead of the notes' baseline.

**States follow NN/g on paper but not in recovery.** NN/g asks for an indicator after about 1 second, and for long waits it asks for step labels, an estimate and a cancel ([progress](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/progress-indicators/)). Errors should appear near their source, with a next step and the user's input kept ([errors](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/error-message-guidelines/)). Material pairs a persistent offline indicator with a Retry snackbar ([offline states](https://m2.material.io/design/communication/offline-states.html)). Nota Bene's "Working..." line carries no phase, no elapsed time and no Stop (`mobile/app/lib/chat/chat_screen.dart:206-215`). Stop is a red 40 dp button 6 dp from Send and fires without confirmation (`mobile/app/lib/chat/composer.dart:349-366`). No snackbar in the app has an action (`mobile/app/lib/chat/composer.dart:79-127`). Server errors reach a stream that nothing listens to (`mobile/app/lib/chat/window_tab.dart:156,171,323-324`). That last one breaks the most basic error rule there is: tell the user.

**Pairing shows the fingerprints and then trusts them anyway.** Signal and Tailscale document QR linking with expiry, explicit approval and device lists ([Signal](https://signal.org/blog/a-synchronized-start-for-linked-devices/), [Tailscale](https://tailscale.com/kb/1336/device-add-qr-code)). Nota Bene's confirm screen shows both fingerprints but offers only Done, and the host is already stored by then (`mobile/app/lib/pairing/confirm_screen.dart:31-52`). The comparison is therefore decorative. The notes found no primary source for a specific fingerprint-comparison UX, but the minimum is obvious: "They match" or "They differ", and nothing is stored until the user confirms a match.

## Accessibility is default Material plus two hand-written nodes

Android asks for labelled long-press actions, merged semantics per group (such as a message bubble), headings, unique pane titles, custom actions for every gesture-only interaction, and cues beyond colour ([Android accessibility](https://developer.android.com/guide/topics/ui/accessibility/principles)). For changing content in Flutter the supported path is `Semantics(liveRegion: true)`. `SemanticsService.announce` is deprecated on Android because it flushes TalkBack's queue ([announce](https://api.flutter.dev/flutter/semantics/SemanticsService/announce.html), [liveRegion](https://api.flutter.dev/flutter/semantics/SemanticsProperties/liveRegion.html)). Android 14 scales fonts non-linearly up to 200%, and Flutter asks apps to test at that maximum ([Flutter migration](https://docs.flutter.dev/release/breaking-changes/android-14-nonlinear-text-scaling-migration)). TalkBack has its own selection gestures, and since 13.1 its actions are reached through reading controls ([TalkBack gestures](https://support.google.com/accessibility/android/answer/6151827)). Against that, the app has `Semantics` in two places (`mobile/app/lib/chat/chat_header.dart:214`, `mobile/app/lib/voice/record_button.dart:212`) and no `liveRegion`, `MergeSemantics` or sort key anywhere. Several touch targets fall below 48 dp, a value the notes sourced only secondarily: 18 dp comment badges (`mobile/app/lib/files/file_markdown.dart:151-155`), 24 dp draft remove, 32 dp quick labels. Labels are 10.5 to 11 px (`mobile/app/lib/chat/window_sheet.dart:430`). The header is clamped at 1.3x (`mobile/app/lib/chat/chat_header.dart:59-61`), so its text never reaches the user's chosen size. The fragment-comment redesign makes this urgent: a model built on selection is inaccessible unless every commentable unit also exposes custom actions.

## Where the notes disagree or stand on thin ground

The sharpest contradiction bears directly on the recommendation. The selection note infers that default-selectable text "matches the reading-app model" and the owner's wish. The chat-apps note infers the opposite: "a design that does not rely on native OS text selection (explicit per-paragraph targets) avoids a known pain point." The recommendation above settles this as B plus a paragraph-level custom-action layer, and says which device results would flip it. Telegram, cited as the fragment-quote champion, does not make selection the default either: you reach it through a tap-opened message view. So the precedent supports fragment actions, not default selection. Default selection rests on reading apps and on complaints, not on any shipping chat app.

Several load-bearing sources are secondary or unopened. All Claude evidence comes from user reports on a third-party aggregator whose pages the researchers could not open (claudeissues.com). The NN/g chatbot findings come from summaries of an 8-participant study. Perplexity's structure comes from teardowns. Gemini's "Select text" and "Ask Gemini" come from an unfetchable Android Authority piece and an APK teardown. WhatsApp's Android partial selection may or may not have shipped. The Codex relay details come from a third-party analysis, and launch details differ across reports. GitHub's long-press multi-line comment gesture is documented only in 2020-2021 sources. Material guidance is M1/M2 throughout, because m3.material.io pages render client-side and returned nothing. That includes tab swiping, the text-selection toolbar and offline states. The 48 dp target, contrast ratios and M3 type scale are unsourced in the notes. Flutter has no first-party Material 3 Expressive, so the floating pill header and tabs are custom either way ([flutter#168813](https://github.com/flutter/flutter/issues/168813)). Two evidence conflicts are unresolved: skeleton screens (one study says they feel slowest, another says fastest; [summary](https://theplusaddons.com/blog/skeleton-loading-screen/)) and the progress-indicator threshold (1 s at NN/g against over 10 s at [UX Tigers](https://www.uxtigers.com/post/progress-indicators)). The notes contain no evidence at all on jump-to-bottom buttons, new-message markers, timestamps, enter-to-send or composer growth in major messengers. Recommendations on those rest on convention, not on sources.

## Gap analysis against the inventory

Priority: **P0** blocks the core loop (answering the agent precisely, and safely, while away from the desk) or is a correctness or security defect. **P1** is a clear deviation from best practice with user-visible cost. **P2** is polish or a lower-frequency path. Numbers in brackets are the inventory's weak points.

## Open questions for on-device testing

Selection mechanics come first, because the main recommendation depends on them. Does a per-item `SelectionArea` in the reversed `ListView.builder` keep its selection when the item scrolls out and back? Does it crash when the item is disposed while it holds a selection edge (the #196102 class), especially while a streaming item above it grows? Does a horizontal drag of a selection handle lose to the `PageView`, with and without the proposed pager lock? Can a word be selected inside a code block, a table or display math, which scroll horizontally, without starting a page turn or an inner scroll? Do long press on text and long press on bubble chrome stay distinguishable at the real bubble padding, so users can find the "more" menu without instruction?

Next is behaviour around the selection. What share of real pending comments quote less than a paragraph? That is the signal for keeping B or moving to A. Do TalkBack users find and use paragraph custom actions through the reading-controls actions menu? Does a polite live region announce "response complete" reliably on Pixel and Samsung TalkBack builds without dropping messages? How do the floating pill, header and composer behave at 200% non-linear font scale and with a code viewer at large font, given the fixed `itemExtent`? Does the deferred permission banner still get approvals answered as quickly as the auto-opening sheet? Is the Stop placement mis-tapped in one-handed use? Finally, on the relay: can push with Approve and Deny actions reach the phone through the self-hosted relay without Google services, and with what latency once Android's background limits apply?

## Conclusion

No phone client in this category has made precise, fragment-level responses to an agent work well. The leading AI apps get user complaints about it, and messaging apps solved it only for short messages. Nota Bene already has the hard parts: comment anchoring, pending-comment batching and a review pane. It is one inverted interaction (selection first, message menu second) away from answering the question the others leave open. That makes the selection rework a differentiator, not just a fix, provided it survives the Flutter risks on a real device. Run that spike before the rest of the P0 work, because its result decides between the two answer models. The rest of the P0 list is cheaper and less glamorous: notifications, surfaced errors and an honest fingerprint check. These make the phone safe to trust while away from the desk, which is the whole reason a companion exists.
